# 20 Nov results: weather, calendar, LightGBM vs LSTM, NPU fusion, live limits

For the team. All tables and the code: [forecast_2025/README.md](../forecast_2025/README.md). Every step:
[WORKLOG_2026-11-20.md](../archive/worklogs/WORKLOG_2026-11-20.md). What's open: [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md).

## In short

1. **The new forecasters clearly beat the deployed ones.** On identical 2025 test weeks the live 1 h grid model is no
   better than persistence (R² 0.862 vs 0.866); LightGBM gets 0.931. At 24 h, grid goes from 0.660 to 0.873 and
   campus solar from 0.677 to 0.724.
2. **Weather forecasts help a day ahead, not an hour ahead:** +0.055 R² for solar 24 h, +0.029 for grid 24 h
   (LightGBM, significant). At 1 h even observed weather adds at most 0.006.
3. **LightGBM is the right model for one year of data:** better than the LSTM in 4 of 8 comparisons, tied in 4, never
   worse. With 1–3 months of history the LSTM is far behind (grid 1 h 0.45–0.54 vs 0.75–0.83).
4. **Didn't pay off:** pre-training on 10 simulated years; giving the grid LSTM last week's load. The guided gate
   helps grid 1 h only (+0.019).
5. **NPU (x86 emulator, not yet on the board):** solar 1 h + 24 h fuse into one graph at no cost (52.9 / 46.5 dB);
   grid passes with a wider calibration set (51.3 / 48.6 dB) but can't share a graph with solar.
6. **The live anomaly check over-flags:** with 2025 limits every model flags 3.4–14 % of normal API readings
   (target ≤ 3 %). API-fitted limits give 1–2 %; they're in the model files, not yet on the board.

## Setup (one paragraph)

Real 2025 meters at 15 min: 6 solar plants per kWp (fault-free days), grid = SB00. Inputs: Open-Meteo forecasts (15-min
for 1 h models, issued the day before for 24 h), clear-sky irradiance, the 2025 academic calendar; ERA5 observed weather
as an upper bound only. Every method on identical rows, R² at +1 h / +24 h, 95 % intervals by resampling test days.
**Blocked** = 8 test weeks spread over the year; **rolling-origin** = each month Mar–Dec predicted from earlier months
only. Models: the baselines, LightGBM, the guided LSTM (2 × 64 body on the NPU, future covariates in a dense head, one
model for all solar plants), and the six deployed models run on the same weeks. The API data are synthetic, so they
only calibrate the live system.

## New vs deployed (blocked)

| | Best baseline | Best deployed | LightGBM | LSTM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grid 1 h | 0.866 | 0.862 | **0.931** [0.91, 0.94] | 0.914 |
| Grid 24 h | 0.654 | 0.660 | **0.873** [0.83, 0.90] | 0.774–0.799 |
| Campus solar 1 h | 0.722 | 0.748 | – | **0.786** [0.73, 0.83] |
| Campus solar 24 h | 0.432 | 0.677 | – | **0.724** [0.64, 0.79] |

The deployed models trained on most of these weeks; on the 3 weeks they never saw, the gaps hold (grid 1 h +0.071,
grid 24 h +0.141 [+0.063, +0.273]).

## Weather

| Weather gain (R², with − without) | LightGBM | LSTM |
|---|---|---|
| Solar 1 h | +0.004 [−0.007, +0.015] | −0.006 [−0.017, +0.004] |
| Solar 24 h | **+0.055 [+0.016, +0.107]** | +0.009 [−0.007, +0.024] |
| Grid 1 h | +0.003 [+0.001, +0.006] | +0.011 [+0.004, +0.021] |
| Grid 24 h | **+0.029 [+0.012, +0.052]** | **+0.062 [+0.019, +0.119]** |

* At 1 h the last readings already carry the clouds: observed (ERA5) weather improves solar 1 h by 0.006 over no
  weather, 0.002 over the forecast. No room.
* At 24 h the gain is knowing tomorrow's sky: LightGBM's skill over the baseline on clear days is +0.52 with forecasts
  vs +0.22 without (cloudy +0.41 vs +0.22). It is larger on unseen months (solar 24 h rolling-origin +0.062 / +0.048).
* Calendar helps grid at 6–24 h (+0.03–0.04); calendar days are still the hardest rows for every model.

## LightGBM vs LSTM

| LSTM − LightGBM (R²) | Blocked | Rolling-origin |
|---|---|---|
| Solar 1 h | −0.005 [−0.012, +0.003] | **−0.019 [−0.030, −0.009]** |
| Solar 24 h | −0.006 [−0.028, +0.013] | +0.007 [−0.007, +0.020] |
| Grid 1 h | **−0.017 [−0.025, −0.011]** | **−0.104 [−0.135, −0.077]** |
| Grid 24 h | **−0.099 [−0.183, −0.040]** | −0.008 [−0.041, +0.027] |

* The LSTM needs data: on grid 1 h it only catches up from July. Unseen seasons cost everyone 0.04–0.16 R².
* We expected the grid 24 h gap to come from the LSTM's 24 h window missing last week's load. Feeding it the day- and
  week-ago load didn't help (−0.024, n.s.), nor did a skip connection. So it's data size, not that input.
* Averaging the two helps only grid 24 h on unseen months (+0.034).
* Grid LightGBM is exported for the board CPU: same test scores after reloading, 0.4–0.6 ms per prediction on a
  laptop core (a similar LightGBM took 2 ms on the board CPU in the 11 Nov benchmark).
* Pre-training on simulated 2015–2024 plants (clear-sky × ERA5 cloudiness × fitted gain): −0.005 / −0.015, n.s.; the
  simulator's day-to-day accuracy is capped by ERA5 (daily R² ≈ 0.57, ceiling ≈ 0.67).

## NPU (x86 HTP emulator, W8A16; target ≥ 44 dB)

| Graph | Per-tensor | **Per-row** |
|---|---|---|
| Solar 1 h + 24 h fused, 100 val windows | 43.5 / 42.0 | **52.9 / 46.5** |
| Solar 1 h + 24 h fused, 500 train+val windows | 37.5 / 43.0 | 40.8 / 49.5 |
| Grid 1 h, 100 val → 500 train+val windows | 33.8 → 46.6 | 34.7 → **51.3** |
| Grid 24 h, same | 37.1 → 43.8 | 37.6 → **48.6** |
| All four fused, 500 train+val (solar 1 h / 24 h / grid 1 h / 24 h) | 36.9 / 42.7 / 19.3 / 26.1 | 40.7 / 49.3 / **19.0 / 26.0** |

* **Per-row weights are what pass**; per-tensor fails solar 24 h.
* **Fuse within a family.** Across families grid collapses: per-row covers the weights, but the shared activation
  tensors keep one scale each.
* **Calibrate per family.** Grid's error sat in 5 of 50 windows (a holiday, a long gap) that the 100 val calibration
  windows never covered; 500 train+val windows fix grid without AdaRound but hurt solar.
* **Board packages built** for solar fused and grid 1 h (`forecast_2025/fused/*_board/`): one script to deploy,
  `b5_score_board.py` to score. Device SQNR and fused/batched timing still need the board.

## Live anomaly limits (M4)

| | Flags on normal API readings | −40 % faults caught |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 limits | 3.4–13.7 % (all fail) | 20–73 % |
| API-fitted limits (out-of-sample) | 0.8–2.2 % (all pass) | 3–70 % |

The earlier pass only looked at November; Feb–May flags 9–35 %. `guided_h4` is the best detector (2.2 % false flags,
70 % of −40 % faults); the 24 h models pass only by going blind (≤ 9 %). The limits are in all six `meta.json` files
(2025 ones kept, `--undo`); pushing them is SYSTEM_DESIGN §9.4.

## What the first pass got wrong

LightGBM and LSTM on different rows; no intervals (most "small gains" are ±0.04); the deployed models never scored on
these weeks; one average hiding where weather matters; the simplifier failure blamed on grid (it hits every graph);
two baseline bugs (missing values scored as 0, no last-week baseline). All fixed in the numbers above.

## Limits

One year of real data (44–55 test days per comparison: < 0.02 R² is noise); every NPU number is from the emulator; the
live feed is synthetic, so weather will help the live demo much less than real data.

## Recommendations

1. **Grid:** LightGBM on the board CPU, 1 h and 24 h. **Solar:** the fused 1 h + 24 h LSTM on the NPU, per-row W8A16
   (the NPU deliverable). Decision pending: [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md).
2. **Before any new model goes live,** the live loop needs a weather fetch (both endpoints work from the board), the
   2026 calendar, the clear-sky table and the new input layout: [LIVE_INPUTS.md](../forecast_2025/LIVE_INPUTS.md).
3. **Next on the board** (once adb works again): push the limits; run both packages and check ≥ 44 dB; time fused vs
   separate calls; a week of shadow mode before promoting.
4. **Keep collecting real 2026 data.** For the LSTM, more history beats any architecture change we tried.
