# Weather, calendar and NPU fusion for campus energy forecasting: what we found (20 Nov 2026)

Worklog with every step: [WORKLOG_2026-11-20.md](../archive/worklogs/WORKLOG_2026-11-20.md). Tables and code:
[forecast_2025/README.md](../forecast_2025/README.md). Live deployment gaps: [forecast_2025/LIVE_INPUTS.md](../forecast_2025/LIVE_INPUTS.md).

## Summary

1. **The new forecasters are clearly better than the deployed ones.** Scored on identical 2025 test weeks, the live
   1 h grid model is no better than "the next value equals the current one" (persistence: R² 0.862 vs 0.866). The
   new LightGBM scores 0.931. At 24 h, grid improves from 0.660 to 0.873 and campus solar from 0.677 to 0.724.
2. **Weather forecasts help a day ahead, not an hour ahead.** At 24 h: +0.055 R² for solar and +0.029 for grid
   (LightGBM, both significant). At 1 h, recent meter readings already carry the weather; even perfect (observed)
   weather adds at most 0.006.
3. **LightGBM is the best model for this amount of data.** Across 8 comparisons (4 targets × 2 test designs) it is
   significantly better than the LSTM in 4 and tied in 4: always better for grid load, tied on solar except 1 h on
   unseen months. With little history the LSTM falls far behind (grid 1 h: 0.45–0.54 vs 0.75–0.83 with 1–3 months of
   data). Averaging the two helps only for grid 24 h on unseen months (+0.034).
4. **Two ideas didn't pay off:**
   * Pre-training on ten simulated years (2015–2024): no gain.
   * Giving the grid LSTM last week's load: no gain at 24 h.
   The LightGBM-guided input gate helps only grid 1 h (+0.019).
5. **NPU:**
   * The solar 1 h and 24 h models fuse into one graph at no cost: 52.9 / 46.5 dB vs FP32, above the 44 dB target.
   * The grid models' poor quantization (34–38 dB) came from a calibration set that held no holidays. Calibrating on
     wider data gives 51.3 / 48.6 dB.
   * All of this was measured in the x86 HTP emulator; the board wasn't connected.
6. **The live anomaly check over-flags.** With limits fitted on 2025 data, every model flags 3.4–14 % of normal
   readings on the 2026 API feed; the target is ≤ 3 %. Limits refitted on API data give 1–2 %. They are now written
   into the model files and take effect on the next push to the board.

A first pass the same day suggested "little improvement anywhere". Most of that came from how it was measured, not
from the models (see "What the first pass got wrong").

## What we tested

Data: real 2025 campus meters at 15 min. The six usable solar plants are normalised per kWp and trained on fault-free
days. Grid is the main substation (SB00). Alongside them:
* Open-Meteo weather: the 15-min forecast for 1 h models, the forecast issued a day earlier for 24 h models, and
  ERA5 actual weather as an unreachable upper bound.
* A clear-sky irradiance model.
* The IIIT-H academic calendar from the 2025 almanacs.

The mentors' 2026 API data is synthetic, so it was used only to calibrate the live system, never for training or
scoring.

Splits:
* Blocked: one test week in each of 8 groups across the year, with gaps around it.
* Rolling-origin: test each month from March to December on a model trained only on earlier months.

All methods are scored on identical rows. 95 % intervals come from resampling test days (day-block bootstrap,
2000 resamples). R² is the value at the horizon: +1 h or +24 h.

Models:
* **Baselines:** persistence, same time yesterday, same time last week, and clear-sky-index persistence (solar).
* **LightGBM:** direct forecast of the value at the horizon from lags, time, calendar, clear-sky and weather forecast.
* **LSTM:**
  * Body: a 2-layer, 64-unit network over the last 24 h; this is the part that runs on the NPU.
  * Head: a dense layer that reads the future covariates.
  * One shared model for all solar plants.
  * An optional input gate initialised from LightGBM's feature importance ("guided").
* **The six deployed models:** run on the same weeks from the dataset they were trained on, with timestamps aligned
  to ours.

## Results

### 1. New vs deployed

| | Best baseline | Best deployed | New LightGBM | New LSTM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grid 1 h | 0.866 | 0.862 | **0.931** [0.91, 0.94] | 0.914 |
| Grid 24 h | 0.654 | 0.660 | **0.873** [0.83, 0.90] | 0.774–0.799 |
| Campus solar 1 h | 0.722 | 0.748 | – | **0.786** [0.73, 0.83] |
| Campus solar 24 h | 0.432 | 0.677 | – | **0.724** [0.64, 0.79] |

The deployed models trained on most of these weeks, so the table favours them. On the three test weeks their weights
never saw, the gaps are similar:
* Grid 1 h: +0.071.
* Grid 24 h: +0.141 [+0.063, +0.273].

### 2. Weather and calendar

| Gain from weather forecasts (R², forecast − no-weather) | LightGBM | LSTM |
|---|---|---|
| Solar 1 h | +0.004 [−0.007, +0.015] | −0.006 [−0.017, +0.004] |
| Solar 24 h | **+0.055 [+0.016, +0.107]** | +0.009 [−0.007, +0.024] |
| Grid 1 h | +0.003 [+0.001, +0.006] | +0.011 [+0.004, +0.021] |
| Grid 24 h | **+0.029 [+0.012, +0.052]** | **+0.062 [+0.019, +0.119]** |

* **Why 1 h shows nothing:** an hour ahead, the last few readings already reflect the clouds. Even ERA5 "perfect
  weather" improves solar 1 h by only 0.006 over no weather (0.002 over the forecast), so there was no room.
* **Where the 24 h solar gain comes from:** knowing tomorrow's sky. LightGBM's skill over the baseline is +0.52 on
  clear target days with forecasts vs +0.22 without; on cloudy days it is +0.41 vs +0.22.
* **Calendar for grid:** +0.03–0.04 at 6–24 h (first pass). Calendar days remain the hardest for every model.

### 3. LightGBM vs LSTM

| LSTM − LightGBM (R²) | Blocked split | Rolling-origin |
|---|---|---|
| Solar 1 h | −0.005 [−0.012, +0.003] | **−0.019 [−0.030, −0.009]** |
| Solar 24 h | −0.006 [−0.028, +0.013] | +0.007 [−0.007, +0.020] |
| Grid 1 h | **−0.017 [−0.025, −0.011]** | **−0.104 [−0.135, −0.077]** |
| Grid 24 h | **−0.099 [−0.183, −0.040]** | −0.008 [−0.041, +0.027] |

* **Weather matters more on unseen months:** solar 24 h rolling-origin +0.062 (LightGBM) / +0.048 (LSTM).
* **The LSTM is data-hungry.** On grid 1 h, trained on 1–3 months, it scores 0.45–0.54 while LightGBM scores
  0.75–0.83. It catches up only from July.
* **Hypothesis tested and rejected:** we expected the 24 h gap to come from the LSTM's 24 h window, which can't see
  last week's load (LightGBM's top grid feature). Feeding the head the load from one day and one week before each
  target step did not help (−0.024, not significant), nor did a skip connection.
* **Practical answer:** grid forecasting runs on the board CPU with LightGBM. The models are exported, reproduce
  their test scores exactly, and take 0.4–0.6 ms per prediction on a laptop CPU. A comparable LightGBM measured
  2 ms on the board CPU in the earlier benchmark.

### 4. Things that didn't help

* **Pre-training on simulated 2015–2024 plant output** (clear-sky × ERA5 cloudiness × a per-plant gain fitted on
  2025): −0.005 / −0.015 R², with intervals that include 0. A lighter recipe chosen on validation picked "no
  pre-training". The simulator's day-to-day accuracy is limited by ERA5 itself (daily R² ≈ 0.57 against an ERA5
  ceiling of ≈ 0.67).
* **Guided input gate:** +0.019 [+0.007, +0.034] on grid 1 h; within noise for the other three models.

### 5. NPU: fusion and quantization (x86 HTP emulator, W8A16)

The emulator reproduced the board within 2 dB on 19 Nov.

| Graph | Per-tensor | **Per-row weights** | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solar 1 h + 24 h fused (one LSTM, block-diagonal weights) | 43.5 / 42.0 | **52.9 / 46.5** | ≥ 44 dB |
| Solar 1 h / 24 h alone | – | 49.4 / 46.4 | |
| Solar 1 h + 24 h fused, calibration 500 train+val windows | 37.5 / 43.0 | 40.8 / 49.5 | |
| Grid 1 h, calibration 100 val windows → 500 train+val windows | 33.8 → 46.6 | 34.7 → **51.3** | |
| Grid 24 h, same | 37.1 → 43.8 | 37.6 → **48.6** | |
| All four fused, wide calibration (solar 1 h / 24 h / grid 1 h / 24 h) | 36.9 / 42.7 / 19.3 / 26.1 | 40.7 / 49.3 / **19.0 / 26.0** | |

* **Fusion costs nothing within a family:** each model keeps its own row scales after fusion.
* **Across families it fails:** with solar in the same graph, grid drops to 19–26 dB (48–51 alone). Per-row scales
  cover the weights, but the shared activation tensors keep one scale each, and grid and solar activations have
  different ranges. Fuse solar with solar; keep grid separate.
* **Calibration has to be chosen per family:** the wide calibration that fixes grid makes solar worse (fused solar
  40.8 / 49.5 dB vs 52.9 / 46.5 with 100 validation windows). Solar 1 h's 40.7 in the all-4 graph is this effect, not
  grid.
* **Per-row quantization is what passes:** per-tensor fails the 44 dB target on solar 24 h.
* **Grid cause and fix:** 98 % of the grid 1 h error came from 5 of 50 test windows, one with a holiday and one with
  a long data gap. None of the 100 calibration windows contained a holiday, so those activations were clipped. A
  calibration set covering them fixes it, without the AdaRound step of the October recipe.
* **Board packages are built** for the solar fused graph (100 validation windows) and for grid 1 h (500 windows):
  an aarch64-android HTP library, 50 test windows and `run_on_board.sh` each, in `forecast_2025/fused/*_board/`.
  Deploying is one script; `b5_score_board.py` then compares board vs FP32 vs emulator.
* **Still needs the board:** timing (one fused call vs separate calls) and batching over plants.

### 6. Live anomaly check (M4)

The live rule flags a reading when it is far from the +15 min forecast, using per-hour limits.

| | Flags on normal 2026 API readings | −40 % faults caught |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 limits | 3.4–13.7 % (all fail ≤ 3 %) | 20–73 % |
| Limits refitted on API data (out-of-sample) | 0.8–2.2 % (all pass) | 3–70 % |

* **Why every model failed:** an earlier pass looked at November only. Feb–May, the feed's high-load season, flags
  9–35 %.
* **`guided_h4` is the best detector:** 2.2 % false flags and 70 % of −40 % faults caught.
* **The 24 h models pass only by going blind:** they catch ≤ 9 % of faults.
* **Limits are written into all six models' metadata.** The 2025 limits are kept in the same file, and `--undo`
  restores them.

## What the first pass got wrong

* **Different test rows:** LightGBM and LSTM were scored on different rows, so their differences were good only to
  about 0.01. E1 now scores everything on the same rows.
* **No uncertainty:** most of the "small improvements" have 95 % intervals of about ±0.04.
* **Deployed models never scored on the 2025 test weeks:** the improvement over them was simply unmeasured.
* **One average hid where weather matters:** the gain is on 24 h forecasts and on telling clear days from cloudy ones.
* **Wrong cause for the simplifier failure:** a QNN conversion failure was blamed on the grid graphs. It hit every
  graph (a numpy/onnxsim mismatch in the container) and is unrelated to grid accuracy.
* **Two bugs inflated or deflated baselines** (fixed): missing values were scored as zeros, and the grid
  same-time-last-week baseline was absent.

## Limits of these results

* **One year of real data:** 44–55 test days per comparison. Differences below about 0.02 R² are not findings.
* **Unseen seasons cost a lot:** scores drop by 0.04–0.16 in rolling-origin tests, most at 24 h.
* **No board measurements:** every NPU number is from the emulator.
* **The live feed is synthetic:** weather inputs will help the live demo far less than they help on real data.

## Recommendations

1. **Grid:** LightGBM on the board CPU, at 1 h and 24 h.
2. **Solar:** the fused 1 h + 24 h LSTM on the NPU, per-row W8A16. This is the NPU deliverable.
3. **Before deploying any of the new models,** the live loop needs:
   * a weather fetch (Open-Meteo; both endpoints tested live);
   * the 2026 academic calendar, which has to be transcribed from the almanac;
   * the clear-sky table;
   * the new input layout.
   Details in [LIVE_INPUTS.md](../forecast_2025/LIVE_INPUTS.md).
4. **When the board is connected:**
   * Push the refitted limits.
   * Run the two board packages and check ≥ 44 dB on the device.
   * Time fused vs separate calls.
   * Run the new models in shadow mode for a week before promoting them.
5. **Keep collecting real 2026 data.** For the LSTM, more history matters more than any architecture change tried
   here.
